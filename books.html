The Books

Four books.
One writer.

Every book is a snapshot of who I was when I wrote it. Some of them feel like distant memories. Some of them still feel like me.

2021
Novel · Young Adult Fantasy

Why The Good Die Young

A sixteen-year-old's imagining of the afterlife, exploring the stages of grief through the lens of a young adult fantasy story.

It was my first novel, written at an age when I was trying to understand grief and what it means to lose someone.

A piece of art formed from grief — and the beginning of everything that came afterwards.

2023
Novel · Dystopian

Blood Brides

A dystopian story about vampires and their control over young women, using the supernatural to explore systems of power.

The novel became my first real attempt at combining two of my biggest fascinations: literature and sociology.

Sometimes the easiest way to talk about society is to make it completely impossible.

2024
Novel · Regency

Cordelia Kills

A Regency-era story about familial pressure, social expectations and what happens when someone refuses to fit the mould they've been given.

At its heart, it is about legacy — and the weight of being expected to carry a family forward when you aren't sure you want to.

A story about the things we inherit, whether we asked for them or not.

2025
Novel · Literary Fiction

Starring Leopold Rigby

A novel that marked a shift in my writing after turning eighteen — more mature, more personal and increasingly interested in the complexity of human experience.

It brought together my interest in literature with my growing fascination with media and storytelling in other forms.

The book where I started to sound a little more like myself.

Growing up on paper

The books changed
because I did.

I started writing novels when I was sixteen. Looking back at those books now feels a little like looking through an old photograph album.

Each one captures a different version of me: the questions I was asking, the things I was afraid of, the ideas I couldn't stop thinking about and the stories I needed to tell.

The genres changed. The characters changed. The writing changed.

But the fascination with people never really did.

I'm still writing. Which probably means I'm still trying to figure something out.

The next story is already somewhere in my head.

I just haven't figured out what it is yet.
